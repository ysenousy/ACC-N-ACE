comprehensive task that transforms architec- tural concepts into safe, functional, and economically viable structures [1]. the task encompasses several interconnected phases: structural system configuration, load analysis, structural analysis, and member design, with code compliance checks throughout the process. tradi- tional design practices often rely on manual structural analysis and design according to codes. with advancements in computing techniques, modern structural design uses an ecosystem of software tools serving distinct yet complementary functions: building information modeling (bim) [2] enables sophisticated visualization and cross-discipline coor- dination; structural analysis software, such as etabs [3] and sap2000 [4], handles complex load and structural analysis; and computer-aided design (cad) software [5] facilitate drafting. the synergy between structural knowledge and computational tools has proven effective in providing reliable and verified design solutions through decades of practical applications. the above structural design approach is challenged by the increasing complexity of modern structures, compressed project timelines, and demands for more sustainable solutions. engineers face mounting pressure to explore alternative design, optimize material usage, and achieve design solutions faster while retaining unwavering safety and code compliance. the evolving challenges have sparked interest in artificial intelligence (ai) applications for structural design tasks. machine learning (ml) approaches have been applied across mul- tiple aspects of structural design: generating and optimizing structural system configurations [6,7], predicting structural responses [8,9], and optimizing structural sizing [10,11]. furthermore, sophisticated ml techniques have shown significant promise in the complex assessment and prediction of seismic performance and risk for reinforced concrete (rc) structures. for instance, recent research has explored optimization- based stacked ml methods and various ml algorithms with advanced hyperparameter optimization for seismic probability, risk, fragility, and vulnerability assessment of rc shear walls and frames. these studies have achieved high accuracy in predicting relevant metrics from extensive datasets generated via incremental dynamic analysis, exem- plifying how ml can be applied to evaluate rc structures and their vulnerability to seismic events based on simulation data [12,13]. the effectiveness of these applications has demonstrated potential to trans- form traditional structural design workflows while enhancing efficiency in time, labor, and cost [14,15]. however, fundamental limitations persist in structural design using black-box ml models with low confine ai applications to research contexts or preliminary design phases. the emergence of large language models (llms) [22] offers new opportunities of structural design. unlike traditional black-box ml models that produce outputs without explanation, llms possess remarkable abilities to understand and generate natural language, reason through complex requirements, and explain their analysis pro- cesses step-by-step, enabling engineers to interact with llms through asking questions, specifying requirements, and receiving detailed ex- planations that mirror how engineers naturally communicate and solve problems [23]. llms have demonstrated promising abilities to under- stand building codes through fine-tuned transformer models [24], automate construction reporting via multimodal frameworks [25], and predict seismic responses using physics-informed approaches [26]. however, llms face a critical reliability limitation that prevents their direct application to structural design due to a fundamental pre- cision problem in two aspects: factual hallucinations when addressing engineering knowledge and computational errors when executing cal- culations [2730]. this dual reliability issue means llms may generate plausible-sounding but incorrect solutions violating code provisions or design principles or making errors in calculations despite following conceptually sound procedures. given that structural design funda- mentally relies on both accurate engineering knowledge and precise calculations to ensure safety, this reliability limitation creates a signif- icant barrier to applying llms to safety-critical tasks. to address these challenges, this paper presents a collaborative multi-agent llm approach based on a framework that breaks down complex structural design tasks into subtasks handled by dedicated llm agents with specific expertise in areas like load and structural analysis, member sizing, or reinforcement design. an llm agent is a specialized instance of an llm assigned with a specific role, expertise, and abilities [31]. rather than modifying the intrinsic architecture of the models, the approach acknowledges these reliability limitations and mitigates them through: (1) integration with external deterministic tools that perform accurate calculations, and (2) generating transparent, step-by-step ex- planations that make the outputs verifiable by human engineers against code compliance and design standards. this human verification is a critical component of the proposed framework, ensuring that safety- critical decisions are subject to professional oversight. the agents are coordinated for information sharing and cross-checking results while maintaining a transparent record of subtasks. this agent-based approach has been inspired by recent research on trip planning [32] and resume screening [33]. the applications of llm agents remain under-explored in the context of structural design. to develop the multi-agent llm approach, key research questions must be answered: how can llm agents be coordinated to perform code- compliant structural design and evaluation? how does the proposed design approach perform? how can the approach be used by practi- tioners? guided by these questions, the objectives of this research are to (1) develop a framework that integrate and coordinate llm agents to collaboratively execute complex design tasks while maintaining trans- parency and verifiability; (2) evaluate the performance of the approach via case studies on structural design and evaluation; and (3) develop an accessible graphical user interface (gui), facilitating practitioners to implement the approach. this research has three main contributions: (1) a new approach is developed to enable code-compliant structural design with automation and minimal hallucination. (2) the concept of multi-agent llm systems is used to develop an ai-powered design framework with high inter- pretability and verifiability. (3) human-robot interactions for reasoning and improving structural design solutions are integrated into the auto- matic design framework, along with an accessible gui. the remainder of the paper is
