as a novel technique to facilitate the conversion of unstructured textual data into a structured computable format. this process is pivotal for extracting meaningful insights and enabling computational systems to understand and manipulate natural language data effectively. by translating the nuances of natural language into a structured representation, nlp systems are equipped to perform a myriad of tasks, including but not limited to, data analysis [17], infor- mation retrieval [18], and the generation of actionable knowledge [19]. this transformative process not only enhances the accessibility of tex- tual data for computational analysis but also serves as a bridge between the ambiguity of natural language and the precision required for algo- rithmic processing. the current information transformation in acc en- compasses a broad spectrum of methodologies using two main approaches: rule-based parsing and machine learning-based parsing. the rule-based approach to information transformation involves the use of a set of predefined, deterministic rules to convert unstructured textual data into a structured format. this approach is grounded in linguistic theories and relies on the explicit programming of language rules which are typically crafted by experts to parse and understand texts. rule-based systems identify specific patterns, keywords, and syntax within the target text, applying these rules to extract and struc- ture information accordingly. the mechanism of a rule-based system typically involves lexical analysis, syntactic parsing, and semantic interpretation, each step governed by a comprehensive set of rules designed to capture the complexity and variability of natural languages. thanks to this, rule-based systems are highly precise for the scenarios they are designed to handle, and the deterministic nature of rule-based systems provides clear traceability and explainability of the decisions made during the information transformation process. in addition, rule- based systems do not require large volumes of annotated data for training, making them suitable for applications with limited data availability. however, the development and maintenance of rule-based systems can be labor-intensive, as it requires extensive domain exper- tise to create and update. as a result, these systems may struggle to adapt to new, unseen examples that do not fit the predefined rules (i.e., the patterns of which were not seen during training). since natural language is inherently ambiguous, rule-based systems may find it challenging to interpret text with multiple meanings without sophisticated disambig- uation rules. in the domain of acc for building codes, some research has been conducted to use the rule-based approach to translate the complex, often legalistic language of building regulations into actionable, machine-readable formats [20,65]. for example, zhang and el-gohary [11] introduced a first-order logic (fol)-based schema that achieves complete automation and reasoning in regulatory compliance of build- ing designs, using ontology-based logic clauses. it reports 100 % recall and precision with perfect information and 98.7 % recall and 87.6 % precision with imperfect information in detecting noncompliance within building designs. zhou and el-gohary [9] proposed an ontology-based information extraction (obie) algorithm that automates the extraction of energy requirements from energy conservation codes with a 97.4 % recall and a 98.5 % precision, showcasing the use of ontology-based pattern matching and sequential dependency methods to handle com- plex provisions and reduce ambiguities. these examples underscore the effectiveness of rule-based systems in specialized domains where pre- cision and clarity are paramount, demonstrating their potential to transform complex legal texts into actionable insights with high accu- racy. nevertheless, the ongoing need for expert input and the challenge of adapting to evolving language and regulations highlight the dynamic balance between the robustness of rule-based approaches and the flex- ibility required for their sustained usability in changing environments. unlike rule-based systems, machine learning-based approach to in- formation transformation enables computers to learn from data, iden- tifying patterns, relationships, and structures within text through statistical methods and artificial intelligence (ai) techniques. the core mechanism of machine learning-based approaches involves several key stages: data preprocessing, feature extraction, model training, evalua- tion, and prediction. compared to rule-based approach, machine learning-based approach can automatically adapt to changes in lan- guage use or domain-specific nuances without manual updates to rules. also, the model can handle large volumes of data more efficiently, making them suitable for applications with extensive datasets. however, machine learning-based approach usually requires a large amount of labeled data, and the performance of models is heavily dependent on the quality of the training data [84]. another disadvantage of machine learning-based approach is that machine learning models, especially deep learning models, can act as black boxes, making it difficult to understand how decisions are actually of facts and rules about objects and their relationships, and queries are made to solve problems by inferring new facts and rules from the existing ones. as an example, picat is a multi-paradigm programming language that is rooted and developed in prolog [32]. in picat, facts are used to represent knowledge in a straightforward way. for example, in a compliance checking system, logic facts can represent buildings characteristics or code requirements: building(officebuilding, height, 70). % height in meters. building(residentbuilding, height, 20). % height in meters. code_requirement(max_height, 60). % height in meters. these logic facts state that: building officebuilding has a height of 70 m. building residentbuilding has a height of 20 m. the required maximum building height in the building code is 60 m. logic rules can also be defined to check if the building complies with the code requirements, for example: height_compliance(building) => building(building, height, height), code_requirement(max_height, maxheight), height =< maxheight. in the rule, height_compliance(building) is the head, which defines the rules name (height_compliance) and its parameter (building). because capitalized words represent variables in picat, the parameter serves as a placeholder for the buildings name or identifier for representing the object that needs to be checked for height compliance. the body of the rule is everything after the => operator (i.e., the body of the logic rule) and consists of three parts, each separated by commas, which represent conditions that must all be evaluated to true for the rule to apply. the first part building(building, height, height) retrieves the height (height) of the specified building (building). it matches against logic facts in the database that represent buildings heights. the fact must have the buildings name or identifier as the first argument, the attribute being checked (height) as the second argument, and the actual height value (height) as the third argument. similarly, the second part code_requirement(max_height, maxheight) retrieves the maximum allowed height (maxheight) from the building code requirements stored as facts in the database. the third part height =< maxheight is a comparison operation that checks if the buildings height (height) is less than or equal to the maximum allowed height (maxheight). if all con- ditions are met, it means the building complies with the height requirement. queries are used to ask the system if the building meets specific code compliance criteria. for example, to check if the officebuilding com- plies with the height requirement, the following query can be used. ?height_compliance(officebuilding). if all conditions in the rules body are satisfied - meaning the buildings height does not exceed the maximum allowed height - the rule concludes that the building complies with the height requirement, and the query will return true (or success). if any condition fails (e.g., if the buildings height exceeds the maximum allowed height), the rule con- cludes that the building does not comply with the height requirement, and the query will return false (or fail). another important concept in programming language is a predicate, which is a fundamental concept that represents a relation among certain terms. each predicate is defined by its name and the number of argu- ments (arity) it takes. for example, in the logic fact building(office- building, height, 70)., building is a predicate with arity 3, indicating an entity and its attributes. in other words, predicates form the basis of expressing both the static knowledge (logic facts) and dynamic in- ferences (logic rules) in a logic program, test was conducted beforehand to assess the equality of variances of each metric between the two tasks. the results of levenes test determined whether the t-test should assume equal or unequal variances. for both the t-test and levenes test, a low p-value (typically <0.05) indicates a significant difference, whereas a high p-value sug- gests no significant difference. additionally, cohens d was used to measure the effect sizes of each metric between the two tasks, indicating the magnitude of the difference [87]. a higher absolute value of cohens d indicates a larger effect size, with thresholds generally defined as 0.2 (small), 0.5 (medium), and 0.8 (large) [87]. table 11 provides a detailed comparison of the models performance in the two experiments. the results show significant automation in transformation of building codes into computable logic programs) through the integration of gpt models and prompt engineering. while the results are promising, several factors influence the generalizability of these findings. first, the framework has demonstrated adaptability and scalability by handling international building codes and car crash reports. however, building codes vary significantly across regions and countries. the frameworks ability to accurately interpret and convert these diverse codes without extensive region-specific training remains to be tested. second, the high precision and recall achieved in the building code translation task with only 2 % of training data suggest its high efficiency. however, the frameworks performance in zero-shot scenarios, when no prior training data is provided, indicates that its effectiveness is closely tied to the availability of domain-specific training data. in regions where annotated data is extremely scarce or costly, this dependence could limit the frameworks 24 automation in construction 168 (2024) 105817 generalizability. third, the practical implementation of the framework in real-world scenarios depends on its integration with existing acc systems. compatibility with current software, ease of deployment, and the learning curve for users could impact its broader adoption and effectiveness. fourth, although the framework reduces the need for extensive manual input, it still requires prompt engineering to adapt the gpt model to specific regulatory contexts. this necessity for expert involvement may still constrain the frameworks generalizability, depending on the availability and expertise of individuals capable of performing these tasks. lastly, while the framework has shown competitive performance compared to rule-based and machine learning- based methods, further comparative studies involving a broader range of the state-of-the-art acc methods and different regulatory domain con- texts are needed to further validate the results. the following limitations are acknowledged in the current study. first, the focus has been primarily on translating quantitative informa- tion from building codes and crash reports, suggesting the need of future work for a broader examination of different information types, such as existential and qualitative requirements in building codes [52], for a more comprehensive understanding of the frameworks capabilities. second, while the framework has been tested on crash reports, specific challenges were encountered during the transformation process. these challenges include handling unstructured and variable data formats and ensuring the accuracy of extracted information. the frameworks applicability and performance in diverse natural language processing tasks beyond building code transformation and crash report trans- formation remains to be fully explored in future research. third, the reliance on a prompt-based methodology means that while the outputs generated are highly interpretable in relation to the specific prompts provided, the underlying mechanisms of the models decision-making process remain opaque, which in the future may be enhanced with explainable ai techniques. fourth, the use of large language models for the framework introduces limitations related to computational re- sources and scalability. llms development requires substantial pro- cessing power, memory, and energy, which can translate to significant costs. additionally, the complexity of network data handling and model maintenance further complicates large-scale deployments based on llms, and may cause data security and privacy concerns as well. future research should explore more efficient algorithms and techniques to address such issues and improve scalability (e.g., through local imple- mentation of small but effective llms), making the framework more accessible for widespread use in the aec industry. finally, the prompt- based methodology, while effective, introduces potential variability in responses from the language model. this variability can affect the consistency and reliability of the outcomes, especially when programming, logic programming, functional programming, and scripting. logic programming in picat central to picat is logic programming, which relies on two main components logic facts and logic rules. logic facts these are basic truths within the system, expressed as predicates. for example, a fact in a family tree program might be john is the father of jane, represented as fatherjohn, jane, the grandfather of z,, enabling the system to infer new facts based on the given rules and facts. working with facts and rules in logic programming, a program comprises a collection of facts and rules. the runtime system uses these to respond to queries. for instance, with the fact john is the father of jane and a rule stating if x is the father of y, then x is a parent of y, a query about john being janes parent would return yes. pattern-matching rules in picat picat utilizes pattern-matching rules, a paradigm that differs from traditional logic programming. in picat, rules are
