case study computational geometric approach for bim semantic enrichment to support automated underground garage compliance checking han gao1; botao zhong2; hanbin luo3; and weiya chen, ph.d.4 abstract: the need for manual semantic annotation to domain-specific building information modeling (bim) models limits the appli- cation of bim technology in various architecture, engineering, and construction fields. bim semantic enrichment refers to a procedure that can identify missing information and improve interoperability across different applications. the need for semantic enrichment of bim models is often emphasized in the literature; however, few studies have shown the potential of semantic enrichment approaches in dealing with problems in real-world cases. to provide an empirical demonstration in the practice, an example of bim semantic enrichment for supporting underground garage compliance checking is selected as a case study. geometric reasoning is one of the effective approaches to enhance semantic information in bim models to support real-world applications. in this study, a computational geometric approach is developed to demonstrate how geometric information can be extracted and used to infer missing semantics in underground garage bim models. a prototype system is developed, and real underground garage bim models are used to validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. the contribution of this study is that it provides the detailed implementation of performing geometric reasoning to enhance bim semantics in real-world cases. additionally, the findings of this case study show that the proposed computational geometric approach has the potential to enhance the semantics of bim models to support decision making in real-world engineering problems, and the developed algorithms can be used to automate the compliance checking process of the underground garage. doi: 10.1061/(asce)co.1943- 7862.0002230.  2021 american society of civil engineers. author keywords: computational geometry; checking aims to identify the defects and short- comings of a specific design before construction transpires (nawari 2020). in the current practice, the code compliance checking pro- cess is usually manually performed by domain experts such as ar- chitects and engineers, the manual process is time-consuming and prone to error. with the development of bim technology, the data are presented in a machine-readable format, and some of the code checking processes have the potential to be automated to save time and provide reliable results. in the past few years, both scholars and industries are trying to establish automated code checking proc- esses in different disciplines. martins and monteiro developed a prototype to perform the hydraulic analysis and the code checking in water distribution network projects (martins and monteiro 2013). choi et al. developed a bim-based evacuation regulation checking system for high-rise buildings (choi et al. 2014). demir altnta and ilal integrated bim and gis data to support automated code checking of building code at a neighborhood scale (demir altnta and ilal 2021). in addition, some checking platforms (such as for- nax, smartcodes, and solibri model checker) have been devel- oped to support some of the building code checking tasks (such as fire safety requirements environmental health requirements) of the government in singapore and the united states (eastman et al. 2009). although there are already considerable automated code checking applications, it only covers a very small part of the build- ing regulations. most efforts on code checking applications are re- lated to fire safety in buildings, but the possibilities of automation for other code checking tasks in practice have not been fully ex- plored. code checking for underground garages is an essential duty before construction. design errors such as the noncompliance of height clearance between pipelines and parking spaces can lead to expensive rework before a garage can be commissioned. but the current compliance checking process for underground garages is at a low level of automation, which entails a great deal of repeti- tive work and is prone to error. bim semantic enrichment various bim applications have been developed to support aec activities. the ifc, which is an open and platform-independent standard (iso 16379), has been established for information inter- action among different aec applications. the ifc format provides the knowledge expression of various entities in the aec field, pro- vides a carrier for the information exchange among different plat- forms, and contains more than 600 entity definitions and 300 type definitions. for example, ifcbuildingelement is a generalization of all elements that participate in a building system, 20 subtypes are used to represent the main building elements (such as ifcwall, ifcdoor, and ifcbeam), and these building elements have their own attributes definitions. ifcrelationship defines the five kinds of re- lationship between objects (composition, assignment, connectivity, association, definition), and the relationships are usually used to provide semantics to support different applications such as indoor pathfinding (fu et al. 2020). however, the ifc schema is not able to cover all building elements and relationships, while some applica- tions like building performance analyses and code compliance checking require higher levels of semantics than the ifc schema can provide (bloch and sacks 2020). bim semantic enrichment refers to the addition of valuable information that cannot be directly obtained in the original digital model (belsky et al. 2016). at present, scholars have developed different bim semantic enrich- ment approaches, and some machine learning-based approaches are developed to ensure the integrity of mapping bim elements to ifc classes. koo et al. extracted the attributes and relationships from the ifc file and used the support vector machine (svm) al- gorithm as a classifier to detect misclassifications of eight kinds of architectural bim elements (koo et al. 2019). similarly, wu and zhang developed a learning-based algorithm to classify building elements (such as beams, columns, footings, and slabs) in an ifc model into predefined categories (wu and zhang 2019). geo- metric reasoning is often used to infer new semantic information from models, lower-level geometry primitives of the 3d model such as the face, polyline, and circle are often used to infer new semantics of objects. for example, pipes can be identified by cur- vature (dimitrov and golparvar-fard 2015), doors and windows can be detected as the empty regions bounded by horizontal and vertical lines (valero et al. 2016). ma et al. applied experts domain knowledge (including geometric features and pairwise relation- ships of objects) to classify bridge objects in 3d models (ma et al. 2018). although much effort has been carried on bim seman- tic enrichment, the goal of most studies is to classify or identify objects to support the detection of misuse of bim objects or 3d reconstruction tasks. few studies have demonstrated the possibility of bim semantic enrichment approaches in supporting engineering decision making in practice. in order to make a better understand- ing of how the semantic enrichment method can be used in engi- neering practice, this paper proposes a computational geometric approach that uses geometric information to enhance the semantics in bim models to support underground garage compliance checking. research enrichment approach apply to support engineering decision making in real-world cases. thus, this study developed a computational geometric approach to enhance semantics in the bim model to sup- port automated compliance checking for underground garages. this study first identified the required information referenced in building code related to the underground garage and then summarized how designers model in most situations from real-world cases and figured out what information should be enhanced in the bim model to support underground compliance checking. afterward, geometric reasoning algorithms were designed based on the observation of characteristics of the bim models. finally, a prototype system was developed to test the effectiveness of the proposed approach in real- world cases. the results show that the proposed method in this study can effectively enhance semantics in underground garage bim mod- els for supporting code compliance checking tasks. the main contribution of this study is two-fold. first, a compu- tational geometric approach is developed to enhance bim seman- tics in this study, and the prototype implementation and test results have shown the potential of the semantic enrichment approach in real-world cases. second, the proposed approach helps improve the level of automation in underground garage compliance checking practice, and it can be adopted by practitioners to solve problems in real-world cases. furthermore, the proposed algorithms can detect other 3d ele- ments with overlapping projection and can be applied to other clear height review
