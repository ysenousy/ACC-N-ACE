introduction structural calculations should be verified with respect to design codes at every phase of the design process. the detailed results are conventionally displayed in the form of two-dimensional drawings. however, the huge dimensions and complicated details of civil structures often lead to conflict in construction work plans based on two-dimensional drawings (leite, 2019). this would be one of the points to resolve for the bim system introduced to construction engineering recently (czmoch and pkala, 2014). unlike a conventional two-dimensional drawing, a bim represents structures in the form of objects in which various digital information is embedded (kong et al., 2022). once a bim is built, various forms of data can be extracted to meet the needs of users, including conventional two-dimensional drawings (kim et al., 2022). quantitative information for structural calculations can be mounted in a bim for various applications. a bim provides an adequate starting point for the development of an automated compliance checking (acc) system for the quantitative verification of structural calculations (teicholz et al., 2018). full-scale research on acc in the architecture, engineering, and construction fields began in the 1960s. the initial concept was to develop a logical process for checking requirements in the design phase. fenves (1966) proposed a compliance checking process for structural engineering that uses tabular decision logic to categorize design variables and values (garrett and fenves, 1987). follow-up studies led to the development of the standards analysis, synthesis, and expression (sase) system for comprehensively checking a structural design against related codes (noland and feng, 1971; fenves et al., 1987; garrett and fenves, 1987). compliance checking through comparison of regulations and standards with accumulated knowledge such as expert systems correspondence goangseup zi  2024 korean society of civil engineers sriram et al., 1984; cronembold and law, 1988; rasdorf and lakmazaheri, 1990; yabuki and law, 1993). the rise of bim intensified acc research. bim can serve as a good data source for acc (preidel and borrmann, 2018). an acc system can be applied to checking all activities related to the design, construction, and maintenance of a structure throughout its service life. this has increased demand for a model that can represent all necessary regulations and codes as well as reflect and apply data from multiple sources or software for different stakeholders (amor and dimyadi, 2021). openbim is a software- independent model that can manage the geometric information of structural components as well as their use, control, and related attributes. it can also contribute to the organization and standardization of various data sources. research on bim-based acc has been mainly based on the industry foundation classes (ifc), which is a standard data schema for openbim (eastman, 2009; eastman et al., 2009; tan et al., 2010; dimyadi and amor, 2013; zhang et al., 2013; malsane et al., 2015; dimyadi et al., 2016). however, the use of acc in structural engineering using openbim is limited. most tools developed for validating structural calculations are built using commercial software (autodesk, 2022a; bentley systems, 2022; skyciv, 2022). these tools are software dependent, which hinders the incorporation of data from other sources. users cannot see how codes are digitalized using these tools (preidel and borrmann, 2018; amor and dimyadi, 2021). these limitations make it difficult for users to follow rule processing in detail. a few commercial software programs offer a transparent approach that allows users to understand how each code applies within rules. solibri model checker (smc) and autodesk navisworks are representative examples of such commercial software (autodesk, 2022b; solibri, 2022). these systems read the ifc model, standard data schema, and allow the user to adjust the parameters of the rules. however, the rules must be coded using functions provided by these systems. the definition of completely new rules within these systems suffers from limitations regarding national regulations and codes (johannes et al., 2016; preidel and borrmann, 2016; zhang et al., 2023). structural design codes often require cross-referencing (rogers, 2023), leveraging results from one code within another. the current hierarchical structure of smc restricts the results of a rule to only rules in the upper layer, hindering flexibility (solibri, 2023). in navisworks, using the results of one rule to check another simultaneously is challenging. a more flexible mechanism for rule execution is needed for acc workflows in structural engineering. research on applying structural design codes to acc is limited and concentrates only on applying a single structural member (patlakas et al., 2018; khattra et al., 2022). the complexity and computational intensity of structural design codes partially explain the limited use of acc systems in structural engineering (patlakas et al., 2018). structural design codes classify structural members based on load (e.g., compression and shear) (kcsc, 2023), whereas bim and ifc categorize parts into physical components. additionally, structural components bear various load types simultaneously. design codes should be selected appropriately considering these loads. additional analysis is needed to bridge the gap between the structural design codes and bim or ifc. the relationship between design variables and each code must also be considered. for example, a small change in the design value of an alignment-related variable will affect not only the bar spacing but also the shear strength and crack control. the relationships between variables and codes should be defined precisely, but this task becomes more complex when the scope expands to the whole structure. the complexity extends to the process of preparing models for acc. these models must represent various design variables for different codes, which requires extensions to the ifc data schema. structural engineering research on bim primarily focuses on integrating structural analysis models into ifc (xu et al., 2019; park et al., 2020; sibenik and kovacic, 2020; jia et al., 2022). these efforts cannot fully address the needs of preparing models for acc. the design variables for acc and those used in structural analysis only partially overlap. acc involves applying various load combinations to structural analysis and comparing only the maximum value of each with the standard value in the code. an acc model also requires a data schema to define standard values that are used to determine whether each analysis result meets code requirements. wu and zhang (2022) proposed integrating building code variables into ifc for acc, but research on extending variables specifically for structural design codes remains limited. in this study, a framework is proposed for developing an acc system to support the verification of structural calculations. the framework systematically applies the information of structural design codes to an acc system in openbim format, focusing on 1) selecting a design code according to the target structure, 2) implementing variables of the design code in ifc format, 3) writing conditions in complex structural code as rules, and 4) designing a mechanism for the acc system to execute all rules simultaneously. this study covers design codes applied during design development in structural engineering. this paper presents the proposed framework, a sample application for the development of an acc system for a prestressed concrete (psc) girder bridge, and verification of the acc system through its application to an actual design example. 2. proposed framework for acc system development 2.1 digital interpretation of design codes most requirements in structural design codes design process. 2.2 implementation of design information in digital format a bim-based acc system uses 3d digital models as the source and design codes for as the target of the compliance check. the models should represent all necessary codes (fenves et al., 1995), but ifc schema cover very limited information related to design codes (zhang and el-gohary, 2016). to add the variables in a design code to an acc system, they need to be defined in the ifc schema. new attributes can be added to the ifc in two ways: defining them as new entities or defining them through property sets (psets). the first approach of adding a new entity deforms the ifc information structure, which makes it difficult to use the ifc file in other applications. in contrast, using a pset to add a new attribute does not change the structure because it uses entities already defined in the existing ifc. psets also allow data to be processed independently of the ifc version of the structural model. therefore, the second approach of using psets was selected in this study. another challenge for acc systems is accessing the objects required by the design code from the ifc model (amor and dimyadi, 2021). this is generally addressed by formalizing ontology-based rules for extracting the required information (zhou and el-gohary, 2016; jiang et al., 2018; zhou and el- gohary, 2018; sydora and stroulia, 2020; zhou and el-gohary, 2021). the ontology is specialized for queries used to find necessary information in a dataset. an ontology representing the ifc dataset is needed. the resource description framework (rdf) query language can be used to express an ontology and has a semantic structure similar to that of ifc. rdf graph data structures are defined according to subject, object, and subjectobject relationships while ifc data structures are defined according to entities, properties within entities, and relationships between entities. rdf graphs can be applied to converting ifc to semantic language for application of other software tools. several converters are applied to convert ifc files to rdf graphs. in this study, the ugentaalto ifc to rdf converter was used (pauwels and terkaj, 2016), which converts ifc to terse rdf triple language (turtle) (beckett et al., 2022). the conversion from ifc to rdf graph is called ifcowl, and it is used to check structural calculations. fig. 6 shows an example of the converted structures, where the entities of ifc are converted by ifcowl to individuals. 2.3 rule interpretation transforming conditions in the design codes from natural language into machine-readable rules is a very important task for the development of an acc system. expert knowledge is needed for accurate interpretation of the design code. rules are used to extract entities from the model and check their values for compliance. the scope of the design code has to be considered; ksce journal of civil engineering 4179 fig. 4. design process of the bridge deck for example, one code may be applicable to the entire structure while another is only applicable to each member. the logical process and exceptions in the code should be identified. design codes have many exceptions and a complicated comparison logic, so understanding the logical flow is very important. next, a language should be chosen for writing the rules. a design code can be converted into a machine-readable form in various ways. as noted earlier, ontology-based rules are used to access data in a model. rule languages are similar in form to natural language, so they are easily encoded by domain experts 4180 w. goh et al. fig. 6. comparison of swrl and sqwrl, and their application to the acc system results. many researchers have used swrl to write design code conditions as machine-readable rules (zhong et al., 2012; zhang et al., 2015; jiang et al., 2018). sqwrl is a swrl-based language with an enhanced query function (o'connor and das, 2009). both swrl and sqwrl consist of two main parts: the antecedent (also known as the body) and the consequent (also known as the head). the antecedent is the if part of the rule. it contains one or more atoms that represent conditions. the consequent is the then part of the rule. it describes the inference or conclusion obtained when the conditions in the antecedent are met. in the acc system, the antecedent conducts compliance checking, whereas the consequent processes the results. swrl and sqwrl differ in the implementation of the consequent, which handles the processing of results. for both swrl and sqwrl in the acc system, the antecedent involves the extraction of the fig. 5. examples of data structures: (a) ifc, (b) ifcowl (jiang et al., 2022). semantic web rule language (swrl) can be used to express rules for an ontology (horrocks et al., 2022). swrl is used to write rules that can infer new knowledge from existing ontologies. in a bim environment, swrl infers whether design variables defined by an ontology (ifcowl) meet relevant code conditions and derives new knowledge, such as review fig. 7. analysis of types and referencing relationships of structural design codes ksce journal of civil engineering 4181 design value from ifcowl and verifying whether the design information in ifcowl satisfies the conditions. depending on whether swrl or sqwrl is used, the review result processed in the antecedent is handled differently in the consequent. for instance, swrl defines the consequent to store new knowledge, such as review results, in ifcowl. sqwrl defines the consequent to display the results in the output window. using swrl or sqwrl to write the ontology rules allows information to be automatically derived through reasoning and querying. as shown in fig. 6, if conditions in the code are written in rule language and are applied to the ifcowl ontology, both methods are applicable. in swrl, the condition is written in the antecedent, and storing the check result in ifcowl is written in the consequent. in sqwrl, the condition is written in the antecedent, but only the output of the check result is written in the consequent. in other words, swrl stores the check result in ifcowl, but sqwrl simply outputs the check result for ifcowl. sqwrl requires additional work to save the check results in ifcowl. however, using swrl requires a longer computation time than using sqwrl. thus, sqwrl was selected in this study. 2.4 process of compliance checking most items in a structural design code may be classified as review or calculation. fig. 7 shows the design code used to verify the shear strength of structural components (kci, 2012). in fig. 7, eq. (7.2.1), highlighted in red, is associated with review-related codes, whereas eq. (7.2.2), highlighted in blue, pertains to calculations for review. review-related codes obtain their results by referencing calculation outcomes. additionally, the variables in eq. (7.2.2) are derived from equations in other codes (e.g., 7.3, fig. 8. input and output structures for rule execution 7.12, and 7.4), which are highlighted in green. therefore, the referencing relationships between these codes should be analyzed to ascertain the appropriate execution order of the rules. our system architecture is designed to use the results of one code within another. the obtained results can then be used as an input for another sqwrl set, as demonstrated in fig. 8. by examining the relationship between the variables used in the rules, we can establish the order for executing sqwrl. ontology-based acc systems generally perform compliance checking through three main functions: an application programming interface (api) for managing and updating the ontology model, an api for rule management, and an inference engine based on the model and rules. owl api (horridge and bechhofer, 2011) is an open-source tool for ontology model management that can be used to load, manipulate, and save ontology files. an acc system can use this api to load ifcowl models containing information about structural calculations. the swrl api can be used for rule management. this api enables sqwrl rules to be processed and manages all rules for checking the conditions in design codes. the compliance checking process reviews rules fig. 9. interpretation of the design process, augmentation of ifc, implementation of rules, and ontology-based acc 4182 w. goh et al. based on calculation and comparison functions built into the swrl api. the open-source drools engine can be utilized as the inference engine, which is the most important part of the acc system (proctor, 2011). the inference engine generates new facts from existing facts based on the stored rules. the engine makes inferences and provides conclusions based on the semantic relationships and rules in the ontology. in other words, sqwrl is executed sequentially using the drools engine, with individuals being created via the owl api following each sqwrl execution. 2.5 framework as shown in fig. 9, the proposed framework for developing an acc system comprises four stages. first, the design process is extracted from actual structural calculations. items to check against the design code at each step are identified. second, the design code is transformed into a digital format, where the code is interpreted as a combination of variables and conditions. third, the data including the variables from the design code and digital information of the structure are converted into the ifc data format, which is transformed further to ifcowl. the conditions from the design code are written in a machine- readable rule language such as sqwrl. finally, an inference engine such as drools is applied to the data and rules. the results of the compliance check can be reviewed by using the ontology editing software protg. 3. development of acc for psc girder bridge 3.1 target structure the proposed acc system was developed and verified using a psc girder bridge as the target structure. its applicability was further confirmed by applying the system to dally bridge in south korea, a psc girder design example, and verifying the review results. the proposed research methodology has two main features. first, a structural design process was employed to determine the design codes applicable to the acc system. applying structural design codes to the acc system required additional efforts to classify the codes for each component of the target structure. this necessity arose because structural components are classified based on loads in structural design codes, but they are classified based on physical components in bim or ifc. the design process in this study effectively bridges this gap. for the studied psc girder, the main components (deck plate, girder, and crossbeam) were identified, and appropriate design codes were selected for each component. second, the execution order of the structural design codes was determined by identifying the referencing relationships between the codes. the acc system is also designed to support mechanisms for using the results of one code in another within a single process. the standard values required for review were calculated and compared with the design values to derive the review results. additionally, the variables constituting these standard values are often determined by referencing other codes, which underscores the strong cross-referencing nature of structural design. thus, the interdependencies of the codes were analyzed, and the execution sequence was determined accordingly. for example, the execution order for the codes applied to the psc girder bridge was identified. the mechanism of the acc system, which handles calculations and reviews simultaneously, enables the concurrent performance of these tasks. this was verified through a comprehensive design review of the dally bridge. 5. conclusions in this study, a framework is proposed for developing an acc system to incorporate structural design codes. a design code can be represented as conditions and variables, which can then be converted into rules compatible with ifc. the proposed framework was verified through its application to the design of the superstructure of a psc girder bridge. the main conclusions are as follows: 1. the design code for the target structure of the acc system was analyzed through the structural design process. structural design codes classify structural members based on their load, whereas bim or ifc categorizes parts based on physical components. the structural design process helps resolve these differences. the conditions that the structure must meet are extracted from the design codes. 2. the design code information can be classified as variables and conditions. these variables can then be defined using psets without modifying the ifc data structure. this enables compatibility with various commercial software. this approach enables the application of structural calculation values to the ifc model of the acc system. the conditions of the design code are converted into rules using sqwrl, and a new function is defined so that a single sqwrl rule can process if else logic simultaneously. 3. the execution order of sqwrl rules is determined by considering the interdependencies between codes. to facilitate ksce journal of civil engineering 4187 the processing of rules for calculation and review in a single process, the compliance-checking mechanism in the acc system was designed to use the results of one code within another. 4. the acc system was developed for the superstructure design of a psc girder bridge. the structural calculation was converted into ifcowl format, and the acc system used sqwrl rules to check the actual design value in ifcowl. the system successfully verified the strength and serviceability of each component, thus confirming the applicability of structural design codes to the acc system. we attempted to establish a general framework for developing acc systems in structural engineering. however, a challenge arose when the target structure of the acc system was expanded. in this field, design codes are interconnected and must follow a specific execution sequence. expanding the scope of the target structure for the acc system complicates the analysis of this execution sequence for all codes. several independent yet interconnected sets of rules should be defined to tackle this issue. the primary function of this acc system is to ensure that structural designs conform to safety regulations and comply with structural design codes. with this acc systems, structural engineers can identify the limits set by these codes and explore various designs within these boundaries. designs can be automatically generated within this acc system, facilitating the use of various optimal designs. these designs can prioritize factors such as cost efficiency and carbon emission reduction. furthermore, the acc system offers the capability to compare the boundaries of design codes across different countries for the same structural components. acknowledgments the authors are grateful to seongwon jung for helping to implement the acc system. this work was supported by the national research foundation of korea (nrf) grant funded
